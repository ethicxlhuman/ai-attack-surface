# Method

## Scope

The subject is the stack a person or team runs to use a model, locally or through third-party tools: the file they download, the template inside it, the software serving it, the network around it, the weights themselves and the agent harness that gives the model hands. An entry belongs here when the attack works against **the person running the stack**.

Out of scope: jailbreaks and refusal removal treated as content-safety problems, training-data privacy, deepfakes and disinformation, and AI used as a tool to attack other systems.

## Layers

A technique lives on exactly one layer: the layer where the attacker's change or entry point sits.

| Layer | The attacker's foothold |
|---|---|
| L1 Model file | The artifact's format, loader or provenance |
| L2 Chat template | The prompt-formatting program shipped with the model |
| L3 Runner | A bug in the inference engine, gateway or its parsers |
| L4 Network exposure | Reachability of an endpoint that should be private |
| L5 Weights + tools | Learned or edited behaviour inside the weights, especially when the model has tools |
| L6 Agent harness | Content the agent reads, add-ons it loads, or the harness's permission design |

## Evidence levels

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `WILD` | A documented incident affecting real users or systems |
| `CVE` | An assigned vulnerability identifier in a named product |
| `PoC` | A published demonstration (paper, write-up or proof of concept) |
| `THEORY` | Described but not demonstrated |

A technique takes the highest level that any of its events supports.

## "First described"

The first event is the earliest **primary** source we could verify that describes the technique as it applies to AI systems. Where a technique has an older root outside AI (pickle deserialization, DNS rebinding, backdoored neural networks in general), the first event is the earliest AI-specific source, and the summary may mention the older root. Where the event is the earliest *we found* rather than an acknowledged origin, its text says "earliest verified".

## Sources

Accepted: peer-reviewed papers and preprints, CVE records and vendor advisories, security-research write-ups by the team that did the work, incident disclosures by the affected party, standards drafts.

Not accepted: videos, podcasts, social posts, newsletters summarising others, AI-generated summaries. They can point us to a primary source but are never cited.

Dates are the source's publication date. A conference paper takes its proceedings date if known; otherwise its preprint date.

## Maintenance

- Every change goes through `data/attack-surface.json`; the layer pages and timeline are regenerated by `scripts/build.py`, which also validates dates, ordering, evidence levels and sources.
- The repository is reviewed against new papers, advisories and incident reports on a regular cycle; each update gets a dated line in `CHANGELOG.md`.
- Corrections (a wrong date, a better primary source, an earlier origin) are as welcome as new entries.
